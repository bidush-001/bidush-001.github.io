Student Mental Health & Academic Success

A statistical research project exploring how university students' mental health, financial stress, and academic pressure relate to their academic performance (CGPA), using EDA, regression, clustering, and classification to uncover insights for universities and student support teams.

Python EDA Regression K-Means Clustering Decision Trees Research

Project Overview

This project explores how university students' mental health, financial stress, and academic pressure relate to their academic performance (CGPA). Using a survey dataset of 87 students, I applied exploratory data analysis, regression, clustering, and classification models to uncover insights relevant to universities and student support teams.

Key Questions

  • Do students with lower GPAs report higher stress or mental health symptoms?
  • Can we group students into risk profiles using clustering?
  • Are stress indicators enough to predict CGPA or performance bands?

Techniques Used

  • Data Cleaning & Feature Engineering
  • EDA with Seaborn / Matplotlib
  • Linear & Polynomial Regression
  • K-Means Clustering
  • Decision Tree Classification
  • Insight-driven markdown summaries

Main Findings

  • Low GPA students reported significantly higher depression symptoms
  • Academic pressure was high across all CGPA groups, not just low performers
  • Clustering revealed distinct student profiles (e.g., high-risk vs. resilient groups)
  • Prediction accuracy was limited (44%), suggesting CGPA depends on broader factors than stress alone

Recommendations

  • Provide targeted mental health support for low performers
  • Promote stress-coping tools across the entire student body
  • Supplement early-warning models with academic history data
